# Awesome Lively Photo Prompt Collection

A curated prompt library for creating believable, natural photographs from a real-person reference image with **Lively Photos Director**.

These are not poster prompts and not generic AI portrait prompts. Each idea is written to preserve a photographic moment: a real person, doing something plausible, in a place that makes sense, under light that could actually exist.

> Use only reference images you own or are authorized to use.

## How to use this collection

1. Upload one or more clear reference photos of the person
2. Pick a prompt below
3. Replace any bracketed placeholders if needed
4. Let Lively Photos Director handle identity lock, optics, lighting, scene plausibility, anti-slop constraints, and QA
5. If you want a set, ask for 3 to 6 variations from the same visual family instead of repeating the exact same composition

## Shared realism direction

Every prompt in this file assumes the following baseline unless you explicitly override it:

```text
Use the uploaded reference image as the identity anchor. Preserve recognizable face structure, skin tone, age impression, hair or head covering, and stable identity traits. Generate a believable photograph rather than a poster or stylized artwork. Keep natural skin texture, plausible anatomy, realistic fabric behavior, coherent shadows, physically believable camera perspective, and contextual props that actually belong in the scene. Avoid stock-photo smiles, plastic skin, dramatic neon lighting, random corporate clutter, fake luxury, extreme blur, excessive symmetry, obvious AI polish, or irrelevant decoration.
```

## 1. Corporate documentary

### 01. Partnership signing

```text
Create a realistic corporate documentary photo of the same person seated at a meeting table during a partnership signing. Include another professional only if needed by the scene. Place a clean presentation screen behind them with restrained contextual branding, with vertical environmental elements framing both sides of the composition. Keep documents and folders in the foreground, soft office lighting, eye-level perspective, moderate depth of field, and natural body language. The image should feel like credible PR photography captured during a real business moment.
```

### 02. Executive meeting, mid-discussion

```text
Create a natural photograph of the same person in a small executive meeting room, caught mid-discussion rather than posing for the camera. They are seated slightly off-center with one hand resting near a notebook and the other making a subtle conversational gesture. Use a believable conference table, ordinary laptops and papers, clean overhead office light mixed with daylight, and a 50mm documentary feel. Keep the room professional but not luxurious or staged.
```

### 03. Reviewing a proposal

```text
Show the same person reviewing a printed proposal at a desk with a colleague just outside the main focal plane. The person is focused on the document, not looking directly into the camera. Use realistic office light, natural paper texture, small signs of actual work on the table, a restrained background, and a medium camera distance. The scene should look like someone quietly photographed a real work moment.
```

### 04. Standing beside a presentation screen

```text
Create a realistic photo of the same person standing beside a large presentation screen while explaining a point to a small group outside the frame. Their posture should be relaxed and natural, with one subtle hand gesture toward the screen. Use realistic meeting-room lighting, readable spatial depth, neutral interiors, no exaggerated stage pose, and no fake futuristic interface graphics.
```

### 05. Walking out of a meeting

```text
Create a candid corporate photograph of the same person walking through a modern office corridor immediately after a meeting, carrying a slim folder or laptop. The camera is a few meters away as if another attendee took the shot casually. Use soft mixed daylight and indoor light, slight natural motion, realistic architecture, and an unposed expression. Avoid fashion-editorial posing.
```

### 06. Quiet boardroom portrait

```text
Create a restrained professional portrait of the same person seated at the end of a boardroom table after a meeting. Keep the table, chairs, and glass surfaces realistic and minimally styled. Use soft side daylight, a 50mm to 85mm natural perspective, subtle facial expression, visible skin texture, and enough background clarity to communicate the environment without turning it into a luxury-office advertisement.
```

## 2. Founder, consultant, coach, and personal brand

### 08. Explaining an idea at a whiteboard

### 09. Thinking between sessions

### 10. Recording a short advice video

Show a normal room, one practical light source, a small desk or chair, and realistic cables or accessories only where they make sense. The camera should observe the recording setup from the side, not produce a polished studio advertisement.

### 11. Client notes after a call

```text
Create a natural desk-side photo of the same person immediately after a client call, writing short notes in a notebook beside an open laptop. ### 12. Personal brand portrait without the pose
```

```text
Create a professional but unposed portrait of the same person standing near a window in a real workspace, looking slightly away from the camera as if someone spoke to them. Use soft side light, natural skin texture, everyday professional clothing, clean but imperfect background detail, and a 50mm lens feel. Avoid crossed-arm founder clichés and oversized smiles.
```

## Prompt recipe

When creating a new idea, this short structure is usually enough:

```text
Create a natural photograph of the same person [doing one believable action] in [one specific real-world environment]. The camera is [distance/angle] using a [35mm/50mm/85mm] natural perspective. Use [one dominant believable light source] and keep [2 to 4 contextual objects] only if they explain the moment. Preserve natural body language, skin texture, anatomy, fabric behavior, shadows, and environmental imperfections. The result should feel observed rather than staged.
```

## Build a visual series, not clones

For a coherent set, keep these constant:

- identity
- general wardrobe family
- lighting character
- color temperature
- photographic mode
- realism standard

Then vary only a few things per frame:

- camera distance
- body orientation
- activity
- foreground object
- exact location within the environment
- gaze direction

This creates family resemblance without making every image look like the same generated template.
