# Examples

These examples define behavior, not fixed visual templates.

## Example 1: corporate signing

User:

> Put me in a formal partnership signing photo, natural and not too staged

Direction:

- mode: documentary-corporate
- action: seated signing moment just before or after signature
- environment: modern meeting room
- context: one justified display or wall mark, folders, pens, table
- composition: controlled symmetry with small human asymmetries
- camera: natural 50mm-style perspective, eye level, medium depth of field
- light: soft neutral office light
- expression: composed, mild acknowledgement of camera, not a broad commercial smile
- avoid: glossy boardroom fantasy, excessive logos, fake handshake, dramatic rim light

## Example 2: explaining work

User:

> I want a photo like I am explaining something to my team

Direction:

- mode: candid-professional
- action: mid-explanation beside a real screen or whiteboard only if it is being used
- composition: subject on a third, gesture moving into open space
- camera: 35mm to 50mm observer perspective
- light: daylight or ordinary workspace ambient
- expression: engaged in the explanation rather than posing
- avoid: everybody staring at camera, random charts, fake neon office

## Example 3: personal brand portrait

User:

> Make this good for LinkedIn but I do not want it to look like an AI headshot

Direction:

- mode: editorial-natural
- environment: simple credible office, studio-natural wall, or window-side context
- composition: chest-up or waist-up, subtle asymmetry
- camera: 70mm-style portrait perspective
- light: soft directional source with restrained fill
- realism: natural skin texture, small asymmetry, normal hair detail
- avoid: blurred luxury lobby, plastic skin, huge catchlights, extreme teeth whitening

## Example 4: conference candid

User:

> Put me at a tech conference talking with people between sessions

Direction:

- mode: event-documentary
- action: active conversation with one or two nearby attendees
- environment: conference corridor or networking zone, event cues visible but secondary
- camera: 35mm to 50mm observer perspective
- light: practical venue illumination
- body language: subject oriented toward conversation partner
- avoid: everybody facing camera, duplicated badges, unreadable giant signage, artificial crowd blur

## Example 5: casual cafe work

User:

> I want it to look like a friend took a photo of me working at a cafe

Direction:

- mode: everyday-lifestyle
- action: working or pausing naturally at a cafe table
- camera: human eye level, 35mm to 50mm feel
- light: window or ordinary cafe light
- props: laptop or notebook only if used
- expression: neutral or mildly engaged, not posed
- avoid: staged influencer breakfast spread, impossible window glow, luxury cafe stereotype

## Negative routing examples

Do not use this Skill for:

- "Make a cyberpunk poster from my face"
- "Turn me into an anime character"
- "Design a product launch key visual with headline and CTA"
- "Create a surreal floating city around me"
- "Make a logo from my portrait"
